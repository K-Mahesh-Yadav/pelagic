The shark's brain

Fourteen senses, ten hidden units, one decision: how hard to turn. Filled circles are firing now; line weight is the strength of a connection. At stage 8 the hidden units start feeding back into themselves, and the brain gains a memory of the tick before.

Sight reaches 160 units and stops, in a 150° cone; nothing is seen to the sides or behind. The four wall senses carry at any distance, on purpose: without them a shark with nothing in view reads a constant, and a constant turn is a circle. The last three senses are the nose: how strong the scent is here, whether there is more of it to one side, and whether it is building up ahead.

What each faculty is worth

Each generation's champion hunts the same water up to three times: intact, with the three scent inputs forced to zero, and with its memory held at zero. Same brain, same fish, same start. The school is adapting too, so expect these to wander.

Keep this brain

Training starts from nothing every time you open this page. Save the weights and you can carry a hunter between sessions, or take it apart offline.

The school is Reynolds' boids: separate, align, follow the centre, and flee a predator. Those weights are no longer fixed — each fish inherits its own, so the school drifts as the shark improves and the shark's catch falls as the school does. Neither side settles.

The seventh number is the one to watch: how far off a straight line away from the shark a fish breaks. Nothing charges for it. Turning sideways simply keeps you nearer the thing chasing you, and turning too far points you back into it, so the price is paid in geometry rather than in energy. Which side a fish breaks to is a coin flip at birth and is not inherited, which is why a school fans apart instead of wheeling as one.

Every fish leaves scent in the water each tick. The field diffuses and decays with a half-life of about five seconds, so the plume behind a school is a record of where it has just been. The shark reads it the way a real one does — by comparing its two nostrils, not by being told a direction.

The brain is a 14–10–1 network with tanh units and 161 weights, ten of which give each hidden unit a leaky connection to its own state one tick earlier — a time constant, so it can average a signal over time instead of only reacting to this instant. Until stage 8 those are held at zero and it is strictly feed-forward, reacting only to this instant. No gradients and no library: weights are mutated and selected on how many fish each shark catches. Senses are handed over one stage at a time, because a brain given everything at once tends to learn nothing.

Because both sides move, a generation's catch is not comparable with one fifty generations earlier: a later shark may be better and still eat less. The trait chart is the other half of that story.
